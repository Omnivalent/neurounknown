Cite & data

NeuroUnknown is a hypothesis-generating resource: it ranks brain-disease genes that have strong genetic evidence but little research, and explains the diseases and treatments around them. It contains no laboratory data and makes no discovery claims. A flagged gene is a starting point for a question, not an answer.

What is in this version

  • 19,299 protein-coding genes scored; 19,203 placed in the gene atlas.
  • 1,366 unique PubMed records screened or cited; 72 references behind the disease, atlas and self pages; 11 clinical guidelines.
  • 7 diseases in 3 groups (neurological, psychiatric, neurodevelopmental) with treatment pathways and drug-site diagrams.
  • A 3D brain with 52 structures and 13 systems.
  • A daily literature watch at 12:00 Central European Time.

Similar resources and how we use them

NeuroUnknown is not the only project that ranks understudied or disease-linked genes. What is distinctive here is the combination: brain-disease genetics, hand-checked literature with dated corrections, the self-disorder focus, and plain-language explanations. We aim to build on these resources, not to replace them.

Pharos / Illuminating the Druggable Genome

Classifies every human protein by how much is known (Tdark, Tbio, Tchem, Tclin).

Now: Linked from every gene in the gene atlas.

Planned: Add the Pharos target development level as an independent 'understudied' measure next to our PubMed count and U score, and report where they disagree.

Understudied proteins (Kustatscher et al. 2022, Nat Methods)

Shows that research attention is concentrated on a minority of proteins and proposes ways to study the rest.

Now: Cited as the main framing for why understudied proteins matter.

Planned: Compare our flagged genes with their understudied-protein lists.

Open Targets Platform

Scores gene-disease associations from genetics, literature, expression and drugs.

Now: Linked from every gene in the gene atlas.

Planned: Import its genetic association scores from the public data downloads as an independent check of our disease-evidence score.

SFARI Gene

Expert-curated autism genes with evidence categories.

Now: Not yet used.

Planned: Cross-check our 216 autism flags against SFARI categories; many may be well known to autism researchers despite few papers.

SCHEMA browser

Exome results for schizophrenia (rare coding variants).

Now: Used: the 10 exome-wide genes and FDR genes define our schizophrenia rare-variant evidence.

Planned: Ingest the full gene table and the 2026 preprint FDR list.

Allen Human Brain Atlas

Gene expression across brain regions in adult donors.

Now: Not yet used (we use the Siletti 2023 single-cell atlas and GTEx).

Planned: Map risk genes onto the self-network regions of the 3D atlas, as a way to prioritise, not as proof of mechanism.

Limitations

  • PubMed record counts measure attention, not knowledge; gene symbols can be ambiguous.
  • Database disease links, especially historical psychiatric susceptibility claims, can be weak; a GWAS locus does not identify the causal gene.
  • RNA in a brain region is not proof that a gene causes a symptom.
  • Treatment pages simplify guidelines for the public and are not prescribing advice.
  • No laboratory validation yet.
