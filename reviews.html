Evidence checks

Rapid reviews

Before expanding the screen further we asked four questions about its own assumptions and checked the published literature on each. The reviews are small and fast by design. They are not full systematic reviews.

How far to trust these. PubMed only (no Embase, Scopus or preprint servers except those PubMed indexes); one AI reviewer, no second screener; decisions from titles and abstracts, not full texts; no risk-of-bias tool; results capped at the top-ranked records for broad searches. Protocols were written before searching (reviews/protocols.md in the repository); where a query was changed after a first look, the change is stated there. Nothing below is a discovery claim, and none of the counts are a measure of study quality.

PubMed searches run 22 November 2026.

What changed in our plan

  • Unstudied-ness (U) must be rebuilt. Review A: the literature defines "unknown" in several incompatible ways, and publication counts are partly predictable from gene properties and affected by publication bias. Our database-only U will be replaced by separate axes (annotation scarcity, PubMed volume, competition).
  • The neuron-only gate is acceptable for schizophrenia and neurodevelopment, not for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's or multiple sclerosis. Review B: the cell-type literature points to neurons for schizophrenia and neurodevelopmental genes, and to microglia and other glia for the neurodegenerative and immune-related conditions. The glial screen (wave 4) stays, and the gate becomes disease-specific.
  • RNU4-2 and RNU2-2 are not a gap. Review C: these small nuclear RNA genes already have dozens of 2024 to 2026 papers. The non-coding blind spot is real, but it is not where an untouched question sits.
  • Our disease score (D) misses rare-variant schizophrenia genes. Audit D: for the genes we checked, XPO7 and CUL1 score D = 0 in our pipeline. Adding schizophrenia exome (SCHEMA) results to D is now on the roadmap.

Review A. How is an "unknown" or "understudied" protein defined?

4no record retrievable
120screened
20included
12excluded after abstract (different concept)
88excluded at title

Findings. (1) There is no single definition. Studies use neXtProt "uPE1" (a protein seen experimentally but without functional annotation; list sizes between about 1,100 and 1,900 depending on release and method), NIH lists such as 162 understudied kinases, or publication counts. (2) Publication counts per gene are largely predictable from a few chemical and biological properties of the gene (PMID 30226837), and understudied genes drop out between genome-wide experiments and the published results (PMID 38546716). So a low count mixes "unknown" with "inconvenient to study". (3) One review warns that paper-mill style knockdown papers may target understudied genes (PMID 30783377), which can inflate counts with low-quality papers; we therefore read abstracts rather than trust counts alone. (4) Many tools predict function for uncharacterised proteins from structure, interaction networks or co-expression, but those are predictions, not validation. (5) Reagents (for example antibodies) and access to model organisms are practical bottlenecks.

What it means for us. Replace the single U with three axes: annotation scarcity (database), PubMed volume, and competition from recent papers. Report the neXtProt uPE1 status of each candidate when available. Our rule-selected waves 3 and 4 already showed that U and PubMed volume agree only moderately (Spearman about -0.55).

Review B. Which brain cell types carry the genetic risk of brain disorders?

21records found
1no metadata returned
20screened
13included
5excluded
2preprints counted once

Findings (title and abstract level). Cell-type enrichment methods exist for heritability, rare variants and eQTLs. For schizophrenia and neurodevelopmental disorders the included studies point to neuronal types and neural progenitors; this matches what the SCHEMA schizophrenia exome study reported (widespread neuronal signal, little glial signal). For Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease the included studies stress microglia and other non-neuronal types (PMIDs 31016231, 39207711), and a cell-type-aware TWAS of Alzheimer's dementia reports 91 risk genes (PMID 42020711).

What it means for us. Keep the neuron-only expression gate for psychiatric and neurodevelopmental tracks. For neurodegenerative and demyelinating tracks use a cell-type-specific gate (microglia, oligodendrocyte lineage, astrocyte), as the wave-4 glial screen already does. Cell-type expression is not evidence that a gene causes a disease.

Findings. The non-coding gene RNU4-2 (a cause of "ReNU syndrome", the name used in the published titles) and RNU2-2 now have many clinical and genetic papers, including saturation editing, cohort and prevalence studies and clinical guidance (records in 2024 to 2026 for these two groups by year: 2024: 9, 2025: 17, 2026: 19). A systematic snRNA analysis and a clinical classification guideline have also appeared. Related minor-spliceosome genes (RNU4ATAC, RNU6ATAC) have an older literature.

What it means for us. Our reconciliation rated RNU2-2 competition "high"; this review confirms it with a broader count. The earlier suggestion of a disease named "ReNU2" is still unverified: the retrieved titles say "ReNU syndrome" for RNU4-2 and describe RNU2-2 disorders by gene name. Non-coding genes need a separate screen; our protein pipeline cannot rank them, and these two are no longer overlooked. Classification by title is a rough tool; the full table is in the download below.

Findings. Nine of the eleven genes have about 100 to over 1,000 records and are not understudied (one more, TRIO, could not be counted reliably).
